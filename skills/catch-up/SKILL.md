# Catch up

The user has not read anything since their last message. Tell them what changed, then list every call that waits on them. Assume they remember their last message and nothing after it.

## Gather

Keep this pass read-only. Use what the session already has:

- your own work since their message;
- reports from subagents;
- messages from anyone else in the session;
- the current state of each pull request, check, or agent the session touched.

Check each number before you show it, and say so when you correct one.

## Write the reply

Open with one sentence that names what waits on the user, or says nothing does.

Spend words on the calls, not on the story. Keep what happened under 100 words, and shorter when less happened. Give each waiting item about 30 words. Never cut an item to fit a count, and never replace an item with a link.

For each item give its link or number, what it changes for the user, the evidence behind your pick, and the risk. Name the pick. When items share one call, write the call and the pick once, then give one bullet per item, riskiest first.

A call is a step only the user can take: a product choice, an approval, an irreversible action, or access you lack. Work you can still do yourself is not a call. A step nobody asked for and the work does not need is not a call.

Say what waiting costs when it is more than a delay, such as a deadline or lost data.

## Visuals

When a change is easier to see than to read, include a screenshot, a short diagram, or a chart rendered from facts you have. Never draw a screenshot you did not capture, and never invent a number. A table is not a visual.
